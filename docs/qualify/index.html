Can the camera certify the part?

The console's release decision took the measurements as given. This chapter asks where they come from. A layer camera watches every layer of a metal print; a witness coupon is pulled apart after the build; a CT scan counts the pores. Each is evidence of a different strength, and a release policy that does not know their strengths is a hope. Three measured answers: a layer camera on an EOS M290 catches , at false alarms per layer; an overheating spot in the heat image is caught above at one false alarm per layer; and an acceptance rule of six tensile coupons catches a percent drop in strength only of the time, which is why qualification is where printing goes to die.

Before the chapter · five things to know

A metal printer spreads a thin layer of powder, melts the part's cross-section into it with a laser, and repeats a few thousand times. Two cameras can watch: a powder-bed camera photographs each fresh layer of powder, and optical tomography integrates the light of the melt over the layer into a heat image.

A recoater streak is a line dragged through the fresh powder by the blade that spreads it; a damaged blade or a part sticking up leaves one. Overheating is a region where the melt got too hot, usually a thin feature or an overhang, and it leaves pores and rough surfaces.

Probability of detection, POD, is the standard way to say what an inspection can see: the chance of catching a flaw as a function of its size, with a confidence bound. The number that matters is a90/95, the size above which the flaw is caught 90 percent of the time, with 95 percent confidence. Below it, flaws pass.

Every detector has a dial between catching more flaws and raising more false alarms. POD means nothing without the false-alarm rate it was measured at, so it is a slider here, not a footnote.

A witness coupon is a test bar printed beside the part and pulled apart afterwards. An acceptance rule says how many coupons, and how strong; the question nobody asks is how often that rule lets a weak build through.

Eight terms used below

LPBF laser powder-bed fusion, the process both datasets come from
EOS M290 a widely used LPBF machine; the layer images here come from one at Aalto University
hit / miss whether a labelled flaw was flagged by the detector at a given setting
a50, a90, a90/95 the flaw size caught half the time, nine times in ten, and nine times in ten with 95 percent confidence
false calls per layer windows flagged on the inspected parts of a layer image where the annotator drew nothing
UTS, yield, elongation the strength at which a tensile bar breaks, the stress at which it starts to deform permanently, how much it stretched before breaking
reference distribution the spread of coupon results from builds known to be good; the yardstick an acceptance rule is measured against
operating characteristic the curve of "probability the rule accepts" against "how bad the build really is"

Act one · What the camera sees

Probability of detection, measured on labelled flaws from one held-out build

Aalto University published layer images from three builds on an EOS M290 in 316L stainless steel, with every recoater streak and every overheated region drawn as a box by hand. A small, transparent detector (four hand-written features and a logistic regression, no neural network) was trained on two builds and scored on the third. Then the hit-or-miss result for each box was fitted with the standard POD model. Pick the camera, then move the false-alarm dial and watch the curve, a90 and a90/95 move with it.

Context, method, limits

How this was built

All numbers are computed by qualify/analysis.py in the repository from the datasets named below, and the page recomputes the POD fit in the browser whenever the false-alarm dial moves, from the per-flaw detector scores and the reference-window score distribution the script exports. The POD model is the hit/miss logistic model of MIL-HDBK-1823A, POD(a) = logistic(b0 + b1 ln a), with a Wald-type 95 percent lower bound; when a detector catches everything or nothing at a setting, the fit is not shown and a binomial lower bound per size bin is used instead. The acceptance-rule curves assume the reference distribution is normal and that a weak build shifts its mean but not its spread.
